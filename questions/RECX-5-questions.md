# Questions for REX AI 5.0 — Kamino (KMNO)

This is the analytical question posed to REX AI 5.0 on RaptorX for the Superteam "ask better questions, find better alpha" challenge. It is grounded in the verified data in `../reports/KMNO-1.md` and is designed to force the report to reconcile its own internal contradiction rather than re-describe the token.

---

## Primary question (the one judged)

> **Context you already hold on Kamino (KMNO):** the protocol generates ~$4.8M in fees and ~$605K in Lend revenue over the trailing 30 days against a ~$1.33B TVL — yet the "Holders Revenue" metric is **$0**, and KMNO governance is documented only as *conditional* ("if/when governance launches"). At the same time, the next unlock (**30-10-2026, ~229.2M tokens ≈ 3.9% of circulating supply**) lands while **~80.3%** of the sample DEX liquidity is concentrated in a single pool (KMNO/USDC on Orca, ~$1.97M) — an executable liquidity base **~540x smaller** than protocol TVL.
>
> 1. Given that executable DEX liquidity (~$2.45M sample) is ~540x smaller than the $1.33B protocol TVL, does the 30-10 unlock represent **net sell pressure** or merely **token availability** — and what concrete on-chain evidence (transfers from recipient wallets, depth near price, slippage) would you require to distinguish the two before calling it "pressure"?
> 2. Which **single** concrete signal in your data would tell me KMNO is a genuine claim on protocol value — as opposed to a points/speculative token decoupled from revenue: confirmed buyback flow, on-chain governance activation, or holder-revenue going non-zero? **Rank them by reliability** and say what you would need to see each one to trust it.
> 3. What **liquidity/TVL threshold** (or slippage bound on the Orca pool) would you require before concluding the 30-10 unlock **cannot** be absorbed without material price impact — and does the 80% single-pool concentration make that threshold materially stricter than it would for a diversified liquidity set?

**Why this is a better question:** it does not ask "what is Kamino" (description) — it points at a specific contradiction in the report (real fees + $1.3B TVL, but $0 holder revenue + only conditional governance + a large unlock into a thin, concentrated liquidity base) and asks for a **decision rule** (a ranked signal, a required evidence set, a numeric threshold), which is what actually separates alpha from noise.

## Secondary question (supporting)

> The report shows two "revenue" numbers that are easy to conflate: protocol **fees** ($4.82M / 30d) vs. protocol **revenue** ($623.5K / 30d), with **Lend revenue** $604.9K and **Holders Revenue** $0. If I treat KMNO purely as a cash-flow claim, the relevant number is holder revenue, not fees. On that basis: **what fraction of KMNO supply is currently at risk of the "fees never reach the token" outcome**, given that the 55% initial stakeholder/contributor allocation is still under a 12-month lock + 24-month vest, and the documented utility is only points-staking? In other words — is the gap between "protocol earns fees" and "token holders earn" a *timing* problem (vesting/governance pending) or a *structural* one (no revenue mechanism designed)?

Pide una regla de decisión (señal rankeada, evidencia requerida, umbral numérico), no una descripción.
